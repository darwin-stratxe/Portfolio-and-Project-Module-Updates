How work moves from enterprise priorities to programmes and projects

Enterprise priorities enter the portfolio as demand. Portfolio management qualifies, compares and authorises the work. Programmes coordinate related projects, projects deliver defined outputs, and operations accepts and sustains the resulting capability and value.

The portfolio is the management layer between strategy and delivery. It controls which work proceeds, when it proceeds and what funding and capacity it may use. Programmes and projects then manage delivery at different levels of detail.
Enterprise Management Framework

Five connected management components

The framework places portfolio management between enterprise and portfolio planning above it, and delivery and operations below it. This establishes the hand-offs the system must control.

ComponentWhat is managedSystem result
1. Enterprise planningStrategic objectives, required outcomes, policy or regulatory commitments, risk appetite and the available funding envelope.A dated set of priorities and constraints that can be linked to every investment request.
2. Portfolio planningWork is grouped around business areas, services or outcomes. Leadership sets the portfolio roadmap and broad capacity allocation.Portfolio boundaries, owners, targets, investment themes and planning periods.
3. Portfolio managementDemand is captured, qualified, prioritised, authorised and continually balanced against funding, people, risk and strategic value.A governed and prioritised portfolio backlog plus a realisation roadmap.
4. Work managementAuthorised work is delivered through programmes, projects, releases or operational work. Scope, schedule, cost, risk, change and outputs are controlled here.Accepted deliverables, outcomes, forecasts, issues, decisions and closure evidence.
5. Operations managementFinance, people, procurement, technology and business operations support delivery, accept the completed capability and track operational performance.Handover, adoption, benefit measures, service performance and lessons returned to planning.

How the five components interact

01Enterprise directionDefines the outcomes, constraints and investment appetite.
02Portfolio planTranslates direction into portfolio themes, targets and capacity envelopes.
03Portfolio decisionSelects and sequences the best combination of work.
04Programme and project deliveryCoordinates outcomes and produces the authorised outputs.
05Operational valueAccepts the capability and measures adoption and benefits.
Feedback moves in both directions: delivery forecasts and capacity limits can cause portfolio rebalancing; benefit results and lessons can change the next enterprise plan.
PortfolioAll current and proposed investments managed together to achieve strategic objectives. The concern is selection, balance, funding, capacity and value.
ProgrammeA coordinated group of related projects and change activity managed together to achieve outcomes and benefits that one project cannot deliver alone.
ProjectTemporary work with a defined scope, delivery approach, baseline and output. The concern is controlled execution and acceptance.
Portfolio management process

From work intake to an active portfolio roadmap

The front end of the system uses one controlled pipeline. A request becomes eligible for investment only after it has been qualified, compared with other demand and approved within the available funding and capacity.

Stage 1 · Work intake

Capture one investment request

A sponsor records the problem or opportunity, intended outcome, beneficiaries, strategic objective, urgency and indicative size. The request remains demand; it is not yet a project.

Manager action
The sponsor creates the request, checks for duplicates, names an accountable owner and submits it for triage.
UI section
Demand & Decisions → Demand capture
Components
Guided form, objective selector, sponsor and beneficiary fields, value statement, cost range, urgency, attachments, duplicate search, save draft and submit.
Control
The system assigns one initiative ID, records the original submission and prevents the requester from treating the item as approved work.
Record state
DraftSubmittedReturnedTriaged
Stage 2 · Work qualification

Test whether the request is viable and ready for comparison

An analyst develops the information needed for a decision: options, expected value, effort, cost, risk, complexity, dependencies, delivery route and capacity implications.

Manager action
The demand analyst coordinates input from finance, risk, technology, procurement and resource managers; the sponsor confirms the completed assessment.
UI section
Demand & Decisions → Investment Assessment Workbench
Components
Inherited request fields, option comparison, estimate class and confidence, risk assessment, dependency register, skills and capacity requirement, benefit profile, review comments and sponsor confirmation.
Control
Author fields and analyst fields remain distinguishable. Missing evidence, unresolved reviews and low estimate confidence are visible in the decision pack.
Record state
Assessment in progressSponsor reviewReady for prioritisation
Stage 3 · Prioritisation and decision

Compare the complete set of demand

The portfolio manager ranks eligible demand against strategic contribution, mandatory commitments, benefit, risk, affordability and scarce capacity. Decision-makers approve a balanced set of work rather than isolated business cases.

Manager action
The portfolio manager builds and compares scenarios. The authorised forum reviews a frozen pack and records approval, conditions, sequencing, deferral or decline with a reason.
UI section
Demand & Decisions → Prioritisation / Investment scenarios / Decision pack
Components
Weighted scoring, rank and contribution view, scenario builder, funding and capacity bars, mandatory-work flags, conflict-of-interest and quorum checks, recommendation, decision, rationale and conditions.
Control
The score supports judgement but does not replace it. Every override and decision stores the authority, meeting, configuration version and rationale.
Record state
PrioritisedApprovedApproved with conditionsDeferredDeclined
Stage 4 · Portfolio roadmap

Admit, structure and sequence approved work

Approval creates an authorised investment. It must then be placed in the correct portfolio, assigned to a programme where work is related, or established as a stand-alone project. The roadmap is rebalanced whenever priorities, forecasts or capacity change.

Manager action
The portfolio manager confirms admission, funding period, delivery route and sequencing. Programme managers confirm whether related work belongs within an existing or new programme.
UI section
Portfolio & Programmes → Portfolio formation / Admission routes / Composition & hierarchy
Components
Admission checklist, portfolio and programme assignment, stand-alone classification, multiyear roadmap, funding allocation, capacity heatmap, dependency map and scenario comparison.
Control
Approved work cannot begin delivery until admission conditions, accountable owners, initial funding and delivery route are confirmed.
Record state
Approved investmentAdmittedSequencedOn hold
Capacity management supports every stage. Resource managers publish available roles and skills, programme and project managers submit demand, and the portfolio manager resolves over-allocation before committing the roadmap.
Programme and project delivery
Delivery level 1 · Programme

Coordinate related projects around shared outcomes

A programme creates the integration layer between the portfolio commitment and multiple delivery teams. It manages the sequence of projects, shared milestones, dependencies, transition activity and the benefit path across the whole change.

Manager action
The programme manager establishes the programme roadmap, assigns projects, resolves cross-project dependencies, coordinates resources and escalates decisions that exceed programme tolerance.
UI section
Portfolio & Programmes → Programme workbench / Roadmap / Integrated master schedule / Dependencies / Benefits architecture
Components
Outcome map, tranche or release plan, project hierarchy, shared milestone view, dependency network, aggregated funding and capacity, programme RAID, benefit map, decisions and transition plan.
Interaction
Receives priorities and constraints from the portfolio; sends delivery forecasts, exceptions and benefit outlook upward; sends scope, interfaces and milestones to projects.
Record state
DefinedBaselinedDeliveringTransitioningClosed
Delivery level 2 · Project

Plan and control the authorised output

The project workspace contains the delivery detail. It turns the approved mandate into a baseline, manages execution and change, produces evidence for oversight and closes only after outputs and residual responsibilities have been accepted.

Manager action
The project manager completes initiation, secures baseline approval, manages work and RAID, submits period updates, raises changes, records acceptance and transfers open benefits and actions.
UI section
Projects → Initiation / Planning / Execution & control / Close-out
Components
Mandate and charter, roles, stakeholders, WBS or backlog, schedule, cost baseline, resource plan, RAID, quality and procurement records, change requests, deliverables, status updates, acceptance and close-out checklist.
Interaction
Receives scope, funding, milestones, tolerances and dependencies from the programme or portfolio; returns actuals, forecasts, risks, changes, outputs and evidence.
Record state
InitiatingPlanningBaselinedDeliveringClosingClosed

The performance domains operate together throughout delivery. They are implemented as connected records and working views, not as eight stages that a manager completes once.

Tailoring is set when the project is admitted and refined at the baseline gate. Project size, uncertainty, criticality and delivery approach determine which fields, artefacts, reviews and approvals are mandatory. The configuration version remains attached to the project record.
Management control · Oversight

Validate performance and manage exceptions

Oversight compares current actuals and forecasts with the authorised baseline. It identifies tolerance breaches, traces the source record and controls the decision to recover, change, pause or stop work.

Manager action
Project and programme managers submit updates; the PMO validates data; finance and resource managers reconcile actuals; governance forums decide material exceptions.
UI section
Oversight & Intervention → Performance / Financials / Resources / Risk / Governance / Exceptions
Components
Baseline-versus-actual trend, schedule and cost forecast, RAG with reason, tolerance panel, dependency and risk alerts, data-quality checks, option analysis, recovery plan, direction and decision log.
Interaction
A project exception can roll up to a programme forecast and change the portfolio roadmap. An approved portfolio intervention flows back down as a new constraint or decision.
Record state
Update dueValidatedException openIntervention activeRecovered
Value level · Operations and benefits

Accept the capability and measure value after delivery

Project closure transfers the capability into operations; it does not end the value obligation. Each benefit retains an owner, baseline, target, measurement date and evidence source after the delivery team has disbanded.

Manager action
The operational owner accepts the handover and adoption plan. The benefit owner records actual results and explains variance. Assurance reviews the evidence and follows corrective actions.
UI section
Value, Assurance & Reporting → Benefits realisation / Adoption & transition / Monitoring & evaluation / Evidence
Components
Operational-readiness checklist, acceptance record, adoption measures, benefit register, baseline and target, actual value, evidence attachment, evaluation schedule, finding, corrective action and lesson.
Interaction
Benefit performance returns to the programme and portfolio view and informs the next planning and prioritisation cycle.

Handover plannedAcceptedBenefit trackingBenefit realisedBenefit closed

PMO capability improvement inside administration

The administration workspace also manages how the PPMIS operating model improves. Each capability is assessed against six factors from the PMO maturity resource, then converted into a prioritised improvement backlog.

Assessment factorManagement use
ImportanceClassify the capability as core, secondary or tertiary for the current operating model.
EffortEstimate the time and cost currently consumed by the capability.
MaturityAssess repeatability and evidence from interviews, records and artefacts.
Cultural alignmentCheck whether the practice is understood, valued and consistently adopted by its users.
Improvement impactEstimate the operational gain available if the gap is addressed.
ConnectivityIdentify the other capabilities, workflows and roles that will be affected by the change.
Manager action
The PMO design authority reviews evidence, sets the current and target state, assigns an owner and approves an improvement intervention and review date.
UI section
PMO Design & Administration → Maturity presets / Capability effectiveness / Improvement backlog
Components
Capability assessment, evidence links, current and target state, importance and impact rating, connectivity map, improvement action, owner, cost and review date.

RecordRelationship

Approved investmentIs admitted to one portfolio and is assigned to a programme or a stand-alone project delivery route.

ProgrammeContains related projects and maintains shared outcomes, milestones, dependencies, transition activity and benefits.

Example: one request moving through the system

  1. A sponsor submits an improvement request in Demand capture. The initiative receives an ID and enters triage.
  2. The demand analyst qualifies it in the Assessment Workbench, with finance, risk, resource and technical input.
  3. The portfolio manager adds it to scenarios and compares its value, cost, risk and capacity demand with other work.
  4. The investment forum approves it with conditions. The decision, rationale, authority and conditions are frozen in the decision pack.
  5. The portfolio manager admits the investment and assigns it to a programme because it shares outcomes and dependencies with existing work.
  6. The programme manager creates the delivery structure, assigns the project, aligns milestones and records the shared benefit path.
  7. The project manager establishes the baseline, delivers the authorised output and submits actuals, forecasts, risks and changes.
  8. The PMO monitors exceptions. If a tolerance is breached, options and a recovery decision move through Oversight & Intervention.
  9. The operational owner accepts the capability, and the project closes after evidence and residual actions are transferred.
  10. The benefit owner continues measurement. Actual value and lessons return to portfolio review and the next enterprise planning cycle.
